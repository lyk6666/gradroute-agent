# Registration guidance

## [policy.registration.online.ay2023] Online registration for the AY2023–24 cohort

The AY2023–24 handbook requires students to register courses online according to the schedule announced by the Office of Academic Services. It does not, in the cited wording, name STARS or promise freshmen pre-allocation. The narrower wording is retained for this cohort rather than backfilling later-handbook language.

## [policy.registration.stars.ay2024-26] STARS registration for AY2024–25 through AY2026–27 cohorts

The AY2024–25, AY2025–26 and AY2026–27 handbooks require online registration through the Student Automated Registration System (STARS), following the Office of Academic Services schedule. They state that freshmen have their first-term courses pre-allocated unless their School gives specific instructions. Each student must use the handbook for their actual intake; a direct-entry student may be directed to the prior cohort handbook.

## [policy.registration.official_attendance] Attend only officially registered classes

Students may attend only classes for which they are officially registered. A student who joins a class without registration is not allowed to sit the course assessments or examination. Attendance, an instructor's informal consent, or a timetable-plan entry is therefore not evidence of registration.

## [policy.registration.outstanding_fees] Outstanding-fee registration bar

Students with outstanding fees are barred from course registration. The handbooks instruct students to clear those fees at least three working days before registering. This is a registration prerequisite, not evidence that payment alone guarantees a place in a course.

## [policy.registration.add_drop] Add/Drop boundary and consequence

Courses may be added or dropped only during the designated Add/Drop period announced by the Office of Academic Services. The public undergraduate schedule identifies Teaching Weeks 1 and 2 as the general full-time Add/Drop window.

A course dropped within Add/Drop does not appear on the official transcript. A student who remains registered after Add/Drop but does not sit the subsequent assessment or examination is deemed to have read and failed the course, with an `F` on the transcript. This rule is not a published route for registration after the deadline.

## [policy.registration.academic_load] Normal load and limited additional load

The selected cohort handbooks describe an expected full-time load of 16–18 AU per semester; the AY2025–26 and AY2026–27 versions expressly note programme variation. Subject to approval by the **Chair concerned**, a student may be allowed up to 3 AU above the normal semester load. Students on Academic Warning, Academic Probation, or with nil CGPA may take only up to normal load.

The handbooks do not identify a public request form, evidence checklist, delegated reviewer or turnaround time. “Chair concerned” is preserved exactly and is not rewritten as “School Chair.”

## [policy.registration.graduation_baseline] Degree-completion baseline

For the selected cohorts, eligibility for a Bachelor's degree requires all of the following: the programme's total AU within its candidature period; all programme course requirements; the minimum residential period; the minimum letter-graded AU; the minimum graduation CGPA; and other prescribed requirements such as internship and applicable language requirements. The programme curriculum and the student's Degree Audit supply programme-specific state; one cohort's totals must not be substituted for another's.

## [policy.registration.matriculation.ay2026-27] AY2026–27 undergraduate matriculation and first-term allocation

Matriculation is the formal act that makes a person an NTU student and carries agreement to University rules and required fees. Matriculated status ceases on completion and degree conferment, withdrawal, or candidature termination. For AY2026–27 undergraduate admissions, the public page gives Semester 1 online matriculation from 9 to 14 July 2026 and a tentative Semester 2 window of 15 to 17 December 2026; late offers follow the page's separate two-working-day instruction.

After matriculation, Semester 1 timetable visibility is expected by end July. Incoming students are told that the School allocates the first-term courses and that they need not register independently unless the School gives specific instructions. A requested first-term change should be discussed with the School's Registration Advisors. The page also says freshmen cannot take first-semester LOA except for extenuating circumstances such as medical reasons, in which case the application goes to the School for review.

## [policy.registration.public_activity_cycle] Public registration and results cycle

The public undergraduate key-activities schedule gives recurring, non-personalised timing:

- Semester 1: registration-schedule release in April, class-schedule release at end May, registration in mid-to-end June, elective allocation results in early August, full-time Add/Drop in Teaching Weeks 1 and 2, and part-time Add/Drop in Teaching Week 1.
- Semester 2: registration-schedule release in October, class-schedule release in mid-November, registration in December, elective allocation results in early January, full-time Add/Drop in Teaching Weeks 1 and 2, and part-time Add/Drop in Teaching Week 1.
- Part-time Special Term: registration-schedule release in January, class-schedule release in early March, registration in mid-to-end March, and Add/Drop from end March to early April.

Exact events for the selected operating year are represented in `academic_calendar.md`; this recurring publication must not be converted into invented exact dates.

## [policy.registration.leave_withdrawal_fee_boundary] Semester leave or withdrawal after Week 2

The public undergraduate key-activities schedule warns that students who apply for Semester Leave of Absence or withdrawal from the University after Teaching Week 2 are liable for tuition and miscellaneous fees for the entire semester. It does not publish the complete LOA or withdrawal decision workflow.

## [policy.registration.stars_guide.functions] STARS Planner and STARS functions in the public version 1.0 guide

The official-public version 1.0 guide distinguishes STARS Planner, used to plan and submit timetables, from STARS, used to register and deregister courses, change or swap an index for the same course, check registered courses and vacancies, and print the timetable. The guide says the planner can save up to three plans and highlights class or examination clashes; a saved plan must still be submitted during the student's assigned registration slot.

The guide also states that registration, deregistration and class-change requests are not accepted through course coordinators or tutors. Because this undated guide uses legacy GERPE/UE labels and old links, these system details are retained as source-version facts, not guaranteed current behavior.

## [policy.registration.stars_guide.waitlist] Waitlist behavior described by the public version 1.0 guide

Within version 1.0 of the guide, Core and Major Prescribed Elective choices in main registration are assigned immediately when a vacancy exists, while GERPE/UE choices are waitlisted. During Add/Drop, all course types are placed on a ranked waitlist and the guide says the University allocates waitlisted courses daily up to the student's allowed maximum load. A successful plan submission does not override an earlier submission; non-clashing courses are added.

This is not evidence of today's priority order, reserved programme quotas, allocation run time, or guaranteed outcome. The source's “vacancy” and “waitlist length” display also does not establish total capacity.

## [policy.registration.incoming_allocation] Incoming CCDS students

The current public CCDS starting-university page says most incoming undergraduates have Core and ICC courses registered automatically. Depending on programme and curriculum, a student may need to register a BDE. Incoming students are told to review registered courses and timetable, check BDE instructions, and monitor NTU email. A suspected registration or timetable issue is routed to the CCDS Course Registration Team; curriculum and academic-policy questions are routed to the CCDS Undergraduate Office.
